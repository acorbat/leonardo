### Work 1: Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia: A National Expert Consensus Statement on Standards of Care

* **Title**: Primary ciliary dyskinesia: a national expert consensus statement on standards of care[1][2]
* **Journal**: *ERJ Open Research*[2]
* **Authors**: Evie A. Robson, Giulia Spoletini, Amelia Bercusson, Siobhan B. Carr, Mary Carroll, Katie Dexter, Lucy Dixon, Claire Hogg, Andrew Jones, Priti Kenia, Michael Loebinger, Jane S. Lucas, Eduardo Moya, Christopher O’Callaghan, Deepa Patel, Daniel G. Peckham, Simon Range, Woolf T. Walker[1]
* **Year**: 2026[2][3]

#### Summary

* **Background &amp; Disease Impact**: Primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD) is a genetically and clinically diverse multisystem disorder characterized by abnormal motile ciliary function or ultrastructure[3][4]. It presents with chronic upper and lower respiratory tract symptoms, bronchiectasis, recurrent sinopulmonary infections, laterality defects (\~50%), congenital heart disease (5–17%), and subfertility in men and women[3][4]. Global genomic analyses suggest a prevalence of at least 1:7500[5]. Diagnostic delays frequently result in inappropriate treatments (e.g., inhaled corticosteroids for presumed asthma) or unneeded surgical lung resections prior to diagnosis[6].
* **Service Model &amp; Delphi Methodology**: In England, PCD care is centralized through National Health Service (NHS) centrally commissioned highly specialised services (HSSs) utilizing multidisciplinary teams (MDTs) composed of respiratory and ENT physicians, respiratory physiotherapists, and clinical nurse specialists[7]. A panel of 15 specialists (8 paediatric and 7 adult respiratory physicians) alongside representatives from PCD Support UK conducted a modified eDelphi consensus process requiring ≥80% agreement to establish 15 minimum standards of care[4].
* **The 15 Agreed Standards of Care**:
  1. **Early Access**: Direct referral and early access for confirmed or highly likely PCD cases to a national HSS management service with specified MDT capacity (100% agreement)[11].
  2. **Specialist Annual Review**: At least annual MDT review (100%), including an ENT consultant review with PCD expertise (100%), and annual audiology reviews for children (at diagnosis or as indicated for adults) (93%)[12].
  3. **Routine Monitoring**: Local or HSS clinic reviews every 3 months for children, and as clinically indicated for adults (100%)[13].
  4. **Microbiology**: Respiratory samples sent at each clinic review; processed via extended culture (cystic fibrosis standards) at annual reviews and as indicated (100%)[13].
  5. **Chest Radiography**: Surveillance chest X-rays considered annually in children; performed as indicated in adults (89%)[14].
  6. **Chest Computed Tomography (CT)**: Performed as clinically indicated; particularly considered upon transition to adult services if no prior CT exists (100%)[14].
  7. **Cardiac &amp; Abdominal Screening**: Echocardiogram and abdominal ultrasound for all patients to identify situs abnormalities or cardiac defects (unless known to be caused by a gene that does not cause laterality defects) (90%)[14].
  8. **Spirometry**: Routine spirometry at clinic and annual reviews for individuals capable of performing the maneuver (93%)[15].
  9. **Extended Pulmonary Diagnostics**: Access to full lung function testing, hypoxic altitude simulation, lung clearance index (LCI), oximetry, and sleep efficiency assessments as indicated (87%)[15].
  10. **Routine Blood Testing**: Full blood count, U&amp;E, LFTs, immunoglobulins, functional antibodies, total IgE, RAST to aeroallergens, and *Aspergillus* markers at diagnosis and as indicated (93%)[15].
  11. **Genetic Testing**: Performed during diagnostic work-up or as soon as possible (100%)[16].
  12. **Airway Clearance**: Individualized airway clearance routine established by a specialist physiotherapist and assessed annually (100%)[16][17].
  13. **Exacerbation Management**: Prompt recognition and treatment of acute pulmonary exacerbations guided by an individualized management plan (100%)[16][18].
  14. **Transition**: Structured transition program ("Ready Steady Go") implemented for adolescents (93%)[16][19].
  15. **Fertility &amp; Genetics**: Fertility discussed during transition, with local access to genetic counseling and fertility services for adults (93%)[20][21].
* **Clinical Monitoring**: Emphasizes risk stratification based on clinical parameters, surveillance for pathogens (*Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Burkholderia cepacia* complex, non-tuberculous mycobacteria), assessment of quality of life (QoL), and participation in research registries[20].

### Work 2: Diagnosis, Monitoring, and Treatment of Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia: PCD Foundation Consensus Recommendations Based on State of the Art Review

* **Title**: Diagnosis, Monitoring, and Treatment of Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia: PCD Foundation Consensus Recommendations Based on State of the Art Review[26]
* **Journal**: *Pediatric Pulmonology*[26][27]
* **Authors**: Adam J. Shapiro, Maimoona A. Zariwala, Thomas Ferkol, Stephanie D. Davis, Scott D. Sagel, Sharon D. Dell, Margaret Rosenfeld, Kenneth N. Olivier, Carlos Milla, Sam J. Daniel, Adam J. Kimple, Michele Manion, Michael R. Knowles, Margaret W. Leigh[26]
* **Year**: 2016[27]

#### Summary

* **Scope &amp; Phenotype**: Developed by North American physicians from the Genetic Disorders of Mucociliary Clearance Consortium (GDMCC) and endorsed by the PCD Foundation, this consensus outlines age-specific manifestations, diagnostic testing panels, and longitudinal care schedules[27]. Major clinical features include[30][31]:
  * **Neonatal distress**: Unexplained respiratory distress in ≥80% of term neonates starting 12–24 hours post-birth with lobar collapse/atelectasis[30][32].
  * **Upper respiratory**: Year-round, daily nasal congestion from early infancy, pansinusitis on CT, and recurrent otitis media with effusion (OME) in ≥80% of children[32].
  * **Lower respiratory**: Year-round, daily wet/productive cough from early infancy, recurrent lower respiratory infections in 80% by preschool, and bronchiectasis in 50% of children by age 8 (universal in adults)[33][35].
  * **Laterality &amp; Fertility**: Situs inversus totalis (SIT) in \~50%, situs ambiguus (SA)/heterotaxy in ≥12%, male infertility (\~100%), and female subfertility/ectopic pregnancy risks[35].
* **Diagnostic Modalities &amp; Cutoffs**:
  * **Nasal Nitric Oxide (nNO)**: Chemiluminescence analyzer value &lt;77 nL/min in cooperative patients aged ≥5 years indicates PCD with 98% sensitivity and &gt;99% specificity (after ruling out CF)[38].
  * **Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM)**: Detects axonemal ultrastructural defects (outer dynein arm [ODA], inner dynein arm [IDA] with microtubule disorganization [MTD], radial spokes, central apparatus) in \~70% of cases[41][42]. Isolated IDA defects require repeat testing[43].
  * **Genetic Panels**: Next-generation sequencing (NGS) of 33 known genes (e.g., *DNAH5*, *DNAI1*, *CCDC39*, *CCDC40*, *DNAH11*, *RSPH1*, *CCNO*, *MCIDAS*) identifies \~50% of cases[44].
  * **HSVA &amp; Immunofluorescence**: Recommended in specialized centers[48][49].
  * **Unrecommended Tests**: Saccharin testing, ciliary beat frequency (CBF) alone, and visual inspection without high-speed recording are discouraged due to high false-positive/negative rates[50].
* **Age-Based Diagnostic Criteria**:
  * **Newborns**: SIT + unexplained term neonatal distress + 1 diagnostic test (TEM, biallelic mutations, or persistent HSVA defect)[54].
  * **1 mo – 5 yrs**: ≥2 major criteria + 1 diagnostic test (TEM, biallelic mutations, or HSVA)[55].
  * **≥5 yrs &amp; Adults**: ≥2 major criteria + nNO &lt;77 nL/min (on 2 occasions, CF excluded) OR TEM OR biallelic mutations OR HSVA[38][55].
* **Clinical Care &amp; Monitoring Schedules**:
  * Pulmonology visits 2–4 times/year with surveillance sputum or cough swab cultures (processed per CF standards including *P. aeruginosa* and NTM)[56].
  * Spirometry 2–4 times/year; CXR every 2–4 years; chest CT considered at least once after age 5–7 years without sedation[57].
  * Pediatric otolaryngology visits 1–2 times/year; baseline audiology; pressure equalization tubes (PET) indicated for conductive hearing loss or speech delay[57][61]. Daily nasal saline irrigations encouraged[58][62].
  * Daily chest physiotherapy and exercise; 2–3 weeks of broad-spectrum oral antibiotics for mild exacerbations; annual flu and pneumococcal immunizations[63]. Selective case-by-case use of inhaled antibiotics, chronic macrolides, hypertonic saline, DNase, or bronchodilators[65]. Inhaled steroids without asthma, IVIG, and routine lobectomy are discouraged[69][70].

### Work 3: Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia: Aetiology, Diagnosis and Clinical Management

* **Title**: Primary ciliary dyskinesia: Aetiology, diagnosis and clinical management[71]
* **Journal**: *Clinical Medicine*[71]
* **Authors**: Rachael Collison, Saara A. Hyatali, Antoniya Kamenova, Adam Rashed, Dylan Riley, Kartik Kumar, Janet M. Stowell, Michael R. Loebinger[71]
* **Year**: 2025[71][72]

#### Summary

* **Aetiology &amp; Pathophysiology**: PCD is an inherited ciliopathy caused by autosomal recessive (and rare X-linked) mutations in over 50 genes (&gt;2,000 mutations) affecting motile 9+2 cilia[73][74]. Global prevalence is estimated up to 1:7,500[73]. Mutations in *DNAI1* (chromosome 9) and *DNAH5* (chromosome 5) encode outer dynein arm components and account for &gt;30% of cases[75]. Genetic testing identifies \~70% of cases, leaving \~30% unknown[72].
* **Clinical Presentation &amp; Course**:
  * Median diagnostic age is 5 years, though many remain undiagnosed into adulthood, where PCD is an underrecognized cause of bronchiectasis[76]. Late diagnosis is associated with reduced baseline FEV1 and higher rates of *P. aeruginosa* colonization[78].
  * Key clinical features: Unexplained neonatal respiratory distress (\~80%), chronic productive cough starting in infancy, persistent rhinosinusitis (half require sinus surgery), situs abnormalities (\~50% situs inversus totalis, dextrocardia), congenital heart disease/laterality defects (25%), recurrent otitis media requiring tympanostomy, and male/female subfertility[78].
  * In adults, bronchiectasis predominantly affects middle and lower lobes[81]. Microbiome transitions over time from early *H. influenzae* and *M. catarrhalis* to chronic *P. aeruginosa* colonization in approximately one-third of adults, as well as NTM risk[82]. Sweat chloride testing is required to exclude CF[82].
* **Diagnostic Strategy**: Diagnosis requires a multi-modal approach incorporating clinical history, nasal NO screening (&lt;77 nL/min cutoff, &gt;90% sensitivity), high-speed video microscopy analysis (HVMA), transmission electron microscopy (TEM), and NGS genetic panels (common genes: *DNAH5*, *DNAH11*, *CCDC40*, *DNAI1*, *CCDC39*, *SPAG1*)[77].
* **Multidisciplinary Management**:
  * Centralized across 4 UK specialist management centers (Royal Brompton, Southampton, Central England [Leicester/Birmingham], and North of England [Leeds/Bradford])[86].
  * Management includes personalized airway clearance (physiotherapy, PEP devices, hypertonic saline/carbocisteine), vaccinations (influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, and functional asplenia protocols), ENT reviews with sinus lavage and FESS, audiology/hearing aids, reproductive medicine, and emerging targeted therapies (idrevloride + hypertonic saline, mRNA gene therapies)[87].

### Work 4: Bronquiectasias no relacionadas con fibrosis quística en niños: guías de diagnóstico, seguimiento y tratamiento

* **Title**: Bronquiectasias no relacionadas con fibrosis quística en niños: guías de diagnóstico, seguimiento y tratamiento (*Bronchiectasis not related to cystic fibrosis in children: Guidelines for diagnosis, monitoring and treatment*)[92]
* **Journal**: *Archivos Argentinos de Pediatría*[92][93]
* **Authors**: Comité Nacional de Neumonología, Sociedad Argentina de Pediatría (Coordinadores: Dra. Silvina Smith, Dr. Maximiliano Salim, Dra. Elizabeth Bujedo; Dra. Gisela Martinchuk Migliazza; Grupo de Trabajo de Kinesiología: Carla Tognini, Yamila Nociti, Sofía Garetto, Maite Olguín Ciancio, Fabiana Pinto; et al.)[92][93]
* **Year**: 2020[92]

#### Summary

* **Scope &amp; Pathogenesis**: Official clinical guidelines from the Argentine Society of Pediatrics addressing non-CF bronchiectasis in children[92].
  * Global annual incidence ranges from 0.2/100,000 in the UK to 17–18/100,000 in Maori/Pacific Islanders and 200/100,000 in Canadian Inuit[96].
  * Based on Cole's "vicious cycle": initial injury impairs mucociliary clearance, resulting in bacterial colonization, persistent inflammation (MMPs, TNF-α, IL-8, neutrophil elastase), and bronchial wall destruction[97]. Early cylindrical bronchial dilation can be reversible with timely treatment[100].
  * Outlines a clinical continuum: Protracted Bacterial Bronchitis (PBB, wet cough &gt;4 weeks resolving with 2 weeks of antibiotics) → Chronic Suppurative Lung Disease (symptoms without HRCT changes) → Bronchiectasis (HRCT-confirmed bronchial dilation)[103].
* **Etiology**: Identified in \~60% of pediatric cases[106]. Leading causes: post-infectious (19%; adenovirus, pertussis, measles, TB, HIV), primary immunodeficiencies (17%), chronic aspiration/foreign body (10%), and Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia (PCD, 7%)[105].
* **Diagnosis &amp; Work-up**:
  * Cardinal symptom is persistent chronic wet cough[94]. High-resolution chest CT (HRCT) is the gold standard diagnostic tool, demonstrating a broncho-arterial ratio &gt;1 ("signet ring sign"), bronchial wall thickening, lack of peripheral tapering, and mosaic perfusion[116]. Pairwise HRCT scans every 3–4 years evaluate permanence/reversibility in children[102].
  * Diagnostic work-up includes FBC, immunoglobulins, HIV serology, sweat chloride testing, swallow/deglutition studies, ciliary function evaluation, and bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) when indicated[117].
* **Management &amp; Exacerbation Protocols**:
  * Exacerbations defined by ≥3 symptoms (increased cough, sputum volume/purulence, dyspnea, wheezing, fever, fatigue, hemoptysis)[120]. Sputum or deep pharyngeal swab cultures guide therapy[121]. Pathogens: non-encapsulated *H. influenzae*, *S. pneumoniae*, *M. catarrhalis*, *S. aureus*, and *P. aeruginosa*[122].
  * Mild exacerbations: Oral amoxicillin/clavulanate or amoxicillin for 10–14 days (azithromycin if allergic)[123]. Severe: IV ampicillin/sulbactam, cefuroxime, ceftriaxone, or piperacillin/tazobactam[124][125].
  * *P. aeruginosa* Eradication: Oral ciprofloxacin or IV piperacillin/tazobactam or ceftazidime + amikacin, followed by inhaled tobramycin or colistimethate[126].
  * Maintenance Therapy: Daily chest physiotherapy (PEP, Flutter, Acapella)[130][131]. Chronic azithromycin (10 mg/kg 3x/week for &lt;25kg, 250 mg 3x/week for 25–40kg, 500 mg 3x/week for &gt;40kg) for 6–24 months reserved for children with ≥3 exacerbations or ≥2 hospitalizations/year, after specialist review and ruling out NTM and QTc prolongation[132]. Inhaled rhDNase is contra-indicated[137]. Salbutamol and ICS/LABA indicated only with confirmed bronchial hyperreactivity or co-existing asthma[138][139]. Full immunizations (influenza, pneumococcal) required[140]. Surgical lobectomy reserved for severe localized disease[141].

### Work 5: Ciliary Videomicroscopy: A Long Beat from the European Respiratory Society Guidelines to the Recognition as a Confirmatory Test for Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia

* **Title**: Ciliary Videomicroscopy: A Long Beat from the European Respiratory Society Guidelines to the Recognition as a Confirmatory Test for Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia[142]
* **Journal**: *Diagnostics*[142][143]
* **Authors**: Noemie Bricmont, Mihaela Alexandru, Bruno Louis, Jean-François Papon, Céline Kempeneers[142][144]
* **Year**: 2021[143]

#### Summary

* **Review Purpose &amp; DHSV Principles**: Analyzes the evolution, diagnostic accuracy, and limitations of digital high-speed videomicroscopy (DHSV) following the 2017 European Respiratory Society (ERS) guidelines[142]. Mucociliary clearance relies on coordinated ciliary beating (normal CBF 10–15 Hz at 37°C)[147][148]. DHSV records ciliated cells at 120–500 frames/second to analyze both CBF and ciliary beat pattern (CBP)[148][149].
* **2017 ERS Guidelines Recommendations**:
  * Weak recommendation to include DHSV (CBF + CBP) in diagnostic work-up[146].
  * Strong recommendation NOT to use CBF alone without CBP analysis[150].
  * Strong recommendation to repeat assessment after air-liquid interface (ALI) cell culture[150].
  * Diagnostic accuracy reported as sensitivity 0.95–1.00 and specificity 0.93–0.95[146].
* **Three Major Unresolved Challenges**:
  1. *Lack of Gold Standard &amp; Efficiency Validation*: TEM + genetic testing miss 20–30% of PCD cases (e.g., mutations in *DRC2*, *OFD1*, *GAS2L2*, *LRRC56*, *CFAP57*, *CFAP221*, *SPEF2*, *DNAH11* exhibit normal TEM ultrastructure)[151]. DHSV identifies these false negatives, but retrospective accuracy studies face methodological criticisms regarding sample selection and secondary dyskinesia controls[151][152].
  2. *Lack of Standard Operating Procedures (SOP)*: High variability across laboratories in sampling methods (brush, curette, forceps), environmental conditions (temperature: 25°C, 32°C, 37°C; pH, osmolarity, media, storage duration)[152]. Studies show CBF increases with temperature, while storage at 4°C or 22°C maintains stability up to 3–9 hours[153]. Long-term culture media choice in ALI also significantly influences CBF[156].
  3. *Lack of Parameter Standardization*: Manual CBP assessment remains highly operator-dependent, subjective, and time-consuming[157].
* **Emerging Automated Technologies**:
  * Computer-assisted software and automated methods are being developed to reduce operator bias: optical flow digital signatures (Quinn et al.), microbead tracking for ciliary fluid shear stress (Bottier et al.), and differential dynamic microscopy (DDM) for spatial/temporal coordination (Feriani et al., Chioccioli et al.)[158]. These techniques require multi-center clinical validation across defined PCD cohorts[159].

### Work 6: Current and Future Treatments in Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia

* **Title**: Current and Future Treatments in Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia[165]
* **Journal**: *International Journal of Molecular Sciences*[165]
* **Authors**: Tamara Paff, Heymut Omran, Kim G. Nielsen, Eric G. Haarman[165][166]
* **Year**: 2021[165]

#### Summary

* **Disease Severity &amp; Current Clinical Evidence**:
  * Inherited ciliopathy (mostly autosomal recessive, rare X-linked or autosomal dominant) causing recurrent airway infections, bronchiectasis, and an average FEV1 decline of \~0.8%/year[167][168]. Genotype severity varies: *CCDC39*, *CCDC40*, *MCIDAS*, and *CCNO* cause aggressive disease (CCNO leads to rapid lung destruction requiring transplantation)[167][168].
  * *N-Acetylcysteine (NAC)*: Oral NAC trial (13 PCD, 44 CF) showed no significant clinical or FEV1 improvement in PCD[169].
  * *Hypertonic Saline (HS)*: First double-blind crossover RCT in 20 adult PCD patients comparing 7% HS vs isotonic saline[170]. HS was safe, improved QoL-B Health Perception scale, but primary total score change was not statistically significant due to underpowering[170][171]. CLEAN-PCD trial evaluated ENaC inhibitor (VX-371) ± Ivacaftor or HS[172].
  * *Recombinant Human Dornase Alfa (rhDNase)*: Not recommended due to potential harm in non-CF bronchiectasis, but pediatric case reports show marked individual benefit (e.g., 14yo girl FEV1 +20%, neonate distress relief, siblings improving)[173][174].
  * *Azithromycin Maintenance*: BESTCILIA phase 3 RCT (90 patients) established that 250–500 mg 3x/week for 6 months halved pulmonary exacerbations (RR=0.45, p=0.004) and reduced pathogenic bacteria[175].
  * *ENT Care*: Chronic rhinosinusitis managed with irrigations, topical steroids, and FESS[176]. OME managed with hearing aids; ventilation tubes remain controversial due to chronic otorrhea (EPIC-PCD study ongoing)[177].
* **Precision &amp; Targeted Future Therapies**:
  * *Gene Therapy*: Ex vivo lentiviral transfer of *DNAI1* cDNA restored outer dynein arms and partial ciliary motility in human cells (Chhin et al.)[178][179]; lentiviral transfer restored *Dnaic1* expression in mouse models (Ostrowski et al.)[180]; adenoviral *IFT88* delivery restored olfactory ciliary structure/function in vivo in ORPK mice (McIntyre et al.)[181].
  * *Gene Editing*: TALEN-mediated gene editing in *DNAH11* mutant PCD cells achieved site-specific recombination in 33% and restored normal ciliary motility in 29% (Lai et al.)[182].
  * *Transcript / mRNA Therapy*: LNP-packaged *DNAI1* mRNA delivered via inhalation expressed protein in mouse lungs/iPSCs (Translate-Bio)[183]; SNIM®RNA platform ETH42 delivering full-length *CCDC40* mRNA (Ethris/Omran)[184].
  * *Read-Through Therapy*: Premature termination codons (PTCs) occur in up to 28% of PCD cases[185]. PTC read-through agents like Ataluren (PTC124) and ELX-02 (Eloxx Pharmaceuticals) are under in vitro evaluation[186][187].
  * *Ciliary Stimulatory Drugs*: Beta-2 agonists, cholinergic agents, ATP, and PDE inhibitors enhance CBF via Ca2+/cAMP pathways and may complement partial ciliary restoration[188].

### Work 7: Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia—Current Diagnostic and Therapeutic Approach

* **Title**: Primary Ciliary Dyskinesia—Current Diagnostic and Therapeutic Approach[189][190]
* **Journal**: *Journal of Clinical Medicine*[189]
* **Authors**: Joanna Wrona, Zuzanna Krupa, Marta Zawadzka, Julia Rydzek, Karolina Dorobisz, Julia Bania[189][190]
* **Year**: 2025[189]

#### Summary

* **Genetics &amp; Structural Mappings**:
  * Autosomal recessive ciliopathy caused by mutations in &gt;40–50 genes encoding motile 9+2 ciliary proteins[191][192].
  * Outer Dynein Arm (ODA) defects: *DNAH5* (most frequent PCD locus), *DNAI1*, *DNAI2*, *DNAL1*, *CCDC114*, *CCDC151*, *ARMC4*, *TXNDC3*, *TTC25*, and *DNAH11* (normal TEM ultrastructure, hyperkinetic/altered beat)[193].
  * ODA + Inner Dynein Arm (IDA) defects: *DNAAF1*, *DNAAF2*, *DNAAF3*, *HEATR2*, *LRRC50*, *DYX1C1*, *ZMYND10*, *SPAG1*, *CCDC103*, *C21orf59*, *C11orf70*, *PIH1D3*, *LRRC6*[194].
  * IDA + Microtubule Disorganization (MTD): *CCDC39*, *CCDC40*, *GAS8*, *RSPH9*, *RSPH4A* (associated with severe lung disease and rapid bronchiectasis)[194][197].
  * Central Pair (CP) defects: *HYDIN*, *RSPH9*, *RSPH4A* (rotational beat pattern, no situs inversus risk)[194][198].
  * Oligocilia (reduced cilia count): *CCNO*, *MCIDAS*[196].
  * Normal TEM phenotype: *DNAH11*, *CCDC164*, *CCDC65*, *RSPH1*[196].
* **Multi-Step Diagnostic Modalities**:
  * Predictive clinical tools: PICADAR (score ≥10 indicates &gt;90% PCD probability), NA-CDCF[199].
  * nNO screening (&lt;77 nL/min cutoff in age ≥5)[200].
  * HSVA: Evaluates CBF (normal 6.3–9.0 Hz at 32°C) and CBP, achieving 96–100% sensitivity and 91–96% specificity when combined[201].
  * TEM: Detects defects in 70–79% of cases[199][202].
  * 3D Electron Tomography: Identifies subtle spatial defects (e.g., &gt;25% volume reduction in proximal ODA in *DNAH11* mutations)[203].
  * Immunofluorescence: Fluorescent staining of ciliary proteins (*DNAH5*, *DNAH11*, *DNALI1*, *GAS8*, *RSPH4A*, *RSPH9*)[204].
  * Culture Models: Air-Liquid Interface (ALI) cultures (e.g., *CFAP300* loss-of-function showing complete immotility), Human Airway Organoids (AOs), 3D Explant Spheroids (3D-E), Conditional Reprogramming Culture (CRC with Y-27632, &gt;90% success)[205].
  * HRCT: Demonstrates bronchial dilation, wall thickening, mosaic attenuation[212].
* **Therapeutics &amp; EPOS 2020 ENT Guidelines**:
  * Infection control: Target *P. aeruginosa* and MRSA. EDTA proposed as anti-virulence adjuvant[213]. Exacerbations treated for 14–21 days[214]. BESTCILIA trial: 6-month maintenance azithromycin reduces exacerbations[214].
  * Airway clearance: NAC, hypertonic saline 7% (improves health perception QoL), dornase alfa (rhDNase - case reports show 20% FEV1 increase)[215]. Exercise yields greater bronchodilation (PEFR increase) than salbutamol in children[218]. PEP devices, vest therapy, chest PT, autogenic drainage[219].
  * Experimental Frontiers: SORT LNP-encapsulated *DNAI1* mRNA inhalation (protein expression sustained up to 24 days)[219]; AAV and lentiviral vectors, CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing[220][221]; *NDUFAF2* mitochondrial protein overexpression and NAD+ boosters (nicotinamide riboside, vitamin B3)[222].
  * ENT Management &amp; EPOS 2020: Classifies PCD as Secondary Chronic Rhinosinusitis (SCRS)[223]. Saline irrigations and intranasal corticosteroids (INCS) recommended[224][225]. Adenoidectomy and FESS reserved for refractory cases[225].
